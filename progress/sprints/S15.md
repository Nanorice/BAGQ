## Retro

### What the hours actually said

Planned 18h, actual 14.5h. The headline is fine; **the composition is the finding**:

| Stage | Type | Budget | Actual | Ratio |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Calculus | refresher, **7 sources / 3 media** | 4h | 5.0h | 125% |
| Linear algebra | refresher, **one source** | 3h | 3.5h | 117% |
| Discrete distributions | **new material**, one source | 3h | 6.0h | **200%** |

**Refresher ≈ 1.2× · new material ≈ 2.0×.** Those two numbers, not the 81%, are what sizes the
next sprint.

### Why the overrun happened

**One-pass reading does not install new material.** The 40-minute input cap was calibrated on
linear algebra — *re-activating* machinery already seen once. Discrete distributions was
*installation* of machinery never held. Ross Ch.4 needed several passes and the stage design gave
no move for "read it, still don't have it", so the second pass happened anyway: unplanned,
unbudgeted, spread across three days. The cap was not wrong; it was applied to the wrong kind of
stage.

**Difficulty triggered drift, and drift had no designated action.** When the material got hard,
attention scattered and both total hours and session contiguity fell. That is downstream of the
first cause: with no planned next move, the only available response to "this is hard" was to
re-read the same paragraph, which is exactly the activity attention will not sustain.

**The fix is structural, not motivational** — name the move: stop, log the unfinished sentence as
a `⚠️ GAP`, switch to Tier A on paper. Paper problems survive low focus.

**Effort was not the problem.** Contact 10 days out of 11.

### What worked, and is now permanent

- **One source per stage.** Calculus at 7 sources fragmented into six sittings; linear algebra at
  one source landed in a single day at full allocation.
- **Splitting distributions into two stages.** Because discrete and continuous were separate and
  self-contained, discrete overrunning did not corrupt continuous — it carried into the next
  sprint clean. As one 8h block the whole thing would have been half-done and unsplittable.
  **Small stages localise overrun.**
- **The named collapse subset** — "if the day collapses, do these two". Cheap to write, changes
  what happens on a bad day.

### What was mis-scoped

Nine distributions in one 8h stage was wrong in both tier and hours — discrete alone came in at
6h, so the full nine were realistically ~12h. Splitting it fixed the blast radius but not the
estimate.

**The rule that came out of it:** any stage whose learning phase will not fit one 90-minute block
gets split *before* it is scheduled, and new material carries the 2× multiplier at estimation
time rather than discovering it at the retro.
