**3. Blockers — root cause, not symptom:**

*Root cause A — one-pass reading does not work for new probability material.* The 40-min input
cap was calibrated on `R.linalg`, a **refresher**: re-activating machinery already seen once.
`F1.4a` was **installation** of machinery never held. Ross Ch.4 needed multiple passes and the
stage design gave no move for "read it, still don't have it" — so the second pass happened
anyway, unplanned and unbudgeted, spread over three days. The cap wasn't wrong; it was applied
to the wrong stage type.

*Root cause B — difficulty triggers drift, and drift had no designated action.* Self-reported:
when the material got hard, attention scattered, so both total hours and session contiguity
dropped. This is downstream of A. With no planned next move, the only available response to
"this is hard" was to keep re-reading the same paragraph, which is precisely the activity
attention will not sustain. **Fix is structural, not motivational:** name the move in the stage
set — stop, log the unfinished sentence as a `⚠️ GAP`, switch to Tier A on paper. Paper problems
survive low focus.

*Not a blocker:* effort. Contact 10/11 days.

**4. What worked (codify into S16):**
- **One source per stage.** Confirmed. `R.calculus` at 7 sources fragmented into six sittings; `R.linalg`
  at one source landed in a single day at full allocation. Keep permanently.
- **Three-block day shape** — held for `R.linalg`. Held for `F1.4a` too; the blocks just needed to
  span two days rather than one.
- **Splitting distributions into two day-stages.** The Day-10 re-plan call was right: because
  `F1.4a` and `F1.4b` were separate self-contained stages, `F1.4a` overrunning did **not** corrupt
  `F1.4b` — it carries into S16 clean. Had they stayed as one 8h `T1.X`, the whole thing would be
  half-done and unsplittable. **Small stages localise overrun.**
- **Named collapse subset** ("if the day collapses: A1, A3, B1"). Cheap to write, changes what
  happens on a bad day.

**5. Circuit breakers triggered (§F)?** **No.** 81% is well above the <60%-for-3-weeks threshold.
Contact 10/11 days, comfortably over the ≥5/week invariant. Nothing to renegotiate.

**6. Was T1.X mis-scoped as Tier 0? — Yes, and the Day-10 split was correct but insufficient.**
Splitting 8h into 2 × 3h fixed the *label* and the *blast radius*. It did not fix the estimate:
`F1.4a` alone came in at 6h, so the original 9-distribution stage was realistically ~12h, not 8h.
**Both the tier and the hours were wrong.** Generalised rule for the rest of the plan: any stage
whose learning phase will not fit one 90-min block gets split *before* it is scheduled, and new
material gets the 2× multiplier at estimation time rather than discovering it at the retro.

**7. Adjustments for Sprint 16:**
- **Adj #12 — Two-pass design for new material.** New-material stages are scheduled with two
  input passes on separate days; Pass 2 opens by re-reading only the logged gaps. Applied to
  `F1.4b` (2.5h + 2.5h + 3h close) and to `S1.1`.
- **Adj #13 — Named move for the drift state.** Every stage set states it: hard + drifting →
  log the `⚠️ GAP`, switch to Tier A on paper.
- **Adj #14 — S1.2 Bayes dropped from S16 → S17.** S16 as written committed 22h (S1.1 12h +
  S1.2 4h + carryover + scrap). With the 2× multiplier and an 11h/week real ceiling, that is
  fiction. S16 = `F1.4b` carryover (Week 1) + `S1.1` (Week 2). Two new-material stages, not three.
- **Adj #15 — S1.1 Combinatorics split into three day-stages** (counting rules / stars-and-bars /
  inclusion-exclusion + derangements), same treatment as distributions. A 12h monolith is a
  `T1.X`-shaped mistake wearing a new label.
- **Adj #16 — `F1.4a` closed as PARTIAL.** Summary table lives in the handnote; solver deferred to
  the shared S16 verifier. The 08-08 +1wk review is the real pass/fail, and it carries the II.1
